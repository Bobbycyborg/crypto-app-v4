INTRODUCTION

Our fear of death
front and center.

A concentrated, streamlined punch — bold, immediate, built to be shared.

This film manages to pack a huge amount into a concentrated streamlined punch – it places our fear of death front and center, shows us how to avoid it, whilst at the same time, being bold and unusual its self, creating honest motivation to actually become something talk about and shared.

This film brilliantly reduces the central idea to a simple conceptual approach, illustrating that we are all harboring a killer inside ourselves. A killer fuelled by a lack of knowledge and submission to instinct. With a resounding clarity our film will solve this, filling that void of information and preparing us to tackle the problem together, united.

It’s a metaphor at it’s purest, without distraction, condensed to form a potent message that is immediate and unforgettable. Instinct isn’t something that happens on a conscious level, it’s an automated self-defense system, usually a positive one, but as with this struggle, there are numerous times it works to our detriment.

When doing physical exercise our body instinctually tries to take shortcuts, avoiding anything it doesn’t have to do. For example, when doing press-ups your body will flare out your elbows and lower your stomach to make it easier. One of the main goals of regular training is actually just removing these instincts.

Another interesting method of undoing instinct is used in the martial art Tai Chi where their basic training is all about undoing the body’s instinct for tensing up and bracing for impact. Instead, their aim is to teach the body to become ultra flexible, fluid and nimble, making it impossible to hit.

These things aren’t common knowledge, people have been imprisoned by their instincts throughout history - despite thinkers from as early as the end of the BC era, like Cicero, who said “The wise are instructed by reason, average minds by experience, the stupid by necessity and the brute by instinct”. Let us be wise and help others to do the same.

VISION.

A great power of this film is the visual journey we’ll go on as spectators, from the empty beauty of a body of water shimmering in the light, to the chaotic beauty of the struggle with its texture of bodies and bubbles, to the epic composed conclusion of everyone laid out in unison.

The underwater world is exquisite, full of light behaving beautifully as the surface distorts, refines and refracts it. The surface it self often creates stunningly beautiful and complex rippling patterns. Not to mention how the contents of the water looks backlit.

The beginnings mass struggle will be an immersive and sobering depiction – gripping the audience with confusion, shock and intrigue. However, this desperate, disorientating chaos will not take anything away from the beauty, in fact it will amplify it, explosions of bubbles will create a thick atmosphere that we’ll shoot through on a shallow depth of field, isolating the bodies amongst billions of out of focus bubbles in front and behind.

The camera won’t have to do much as all the chaos will come from the action but I’d also like to explore mimicking the chaos with the camera. We’ll capture some of the plunges from underneath where the camera will jerk deeper as the bodies plummet towards it. At the end the camera drifts or floats as if it’s levitating, mirroring the calmness of the survivors.

The tranquility of these moments (as well as the very opening shot) will be equally mesmerizingly beautiful – full of shafts of light, mosaic patterns (on the underside of the surface) and a haze of texture.

Throughout, intimate close ups connect us with individuals and their reactions to the brutal cold water, whilst epic wide shots illustrate the scale of the problem. Surreal moments of slow motion with exaggerated sound design will punctuate the loud frantic chaos so our important moments hit home with crystal clarity.

Something else I’d like to look at is rotating the camera during the beginning of the film, adding to the chaos but crucially setting it up to stop rotating by the end of the film, coming to rest on everyone’s starfish shapes, subtly conveying a sense of truth and conclusion to what you seeing.

We could also try gradually moving away from shots of individuals as the film progresses and showing everything in groups, until we finish with shot of starfish’s en mass, illustrating the power of collective knowledge.

It will be a feast of rich, cinematic beauty - stunningly elegant and captivating.

TONE.

As well as a visual journey, we will also be guiding our audience through a voyage in tone, from the hectic chaos of an action movie to the calm, reflective, thoughtful and intelligent tone of a drama. We’ll combine the situations honest and brutal reality with the more conceptual and surreal vibe that the script feels full of.

Finding oneself dropping into the middle of a freezing deep body of water would be a terrifying ordeal and I think we should show this. The struggle, which leads to death, would be an ordeal and we should feel this in the eyes of our swimmers, and, because it’s what will really connect with the audience.

And likewise at the end, I’d imagine that this is their first time almost dying in freezing water (hence the initial struggle) so when they take the decision to stop struggling and wait. There is an element of not knowing if this is going to work, it’s a risk and it would be terrifying.

It’s not like they’re suddenly touched by a branch of knowledge and they know it will work beyond any doubt. Even if this was the case, they’re still in ice-cold water, which hurts; it’s literally painful and seeing this integral to the audience buying it.

I probably sound slightly sadistic here but I think their performance is of paramount importance and a critical part of the orchestra, with everything else in the film.

In the meeting we touched on whether they actually notice each other stop struggling and spread out to the star position. This could have a surrealism to it that makes the message even more potent and pure. Although, it’s hard to truly know at this stage, so I’d plan to cover both options, shooting some looks exchanged amongst them and give us a choice in the edit.
