  • Field Notes

My first camera was my dad's old Nikon, kept in a biscuit tin with a seaweed smell from the garage. I learned focus on the pier at Lyme Regis before I learned it in school.

Documentary work is mostly waiting: fog on the windscreen, tea in a paper cup, the same question asked twice because someone needed time to trust you.

I still print contact sheets. Crooked frames and light leaks remind me the edit is a conversation, not a verdict.

A season with the RNLI changed how I read weather. They do not perform for the lens; you earn every frame or you get nothing.

On long drives I listen to This American Life — stories told slowly, the way a good portrait should feel.

I travel with one body and a 35mm lens. Less gear means more attention to what is actually happening in the room.

Martin Parr once signed a book for me with the line look harder. I keep that in the bag with the spare batteries.

Stephen Howse is a British photographer working across performance, documentary and portrait. He is based on the south coast and travels for commissions, festivals and long form stories. The work is quiet and patient: arrive early, stay late, and let people forget the camera is there. Stephen began assisting theatre photographers before moving into editorial and charity campaigns. His pictures favour available light, close distance and honesty over styling. Recent work includes lifeboat crews, backstage rehearsal rooms, touring musicians and portraits of people who would rather be doing something else than sitting for a photograph. He has worked with theatres, newspapers, galleries and NGOs. Prints are available on request. Assignments and collaborations are welcome.
